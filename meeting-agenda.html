010:00

What we're actually building

3 minutes
Land this: this is a keeper league, not a redraft and not a dynasty. You hold a few players for a few years, at a price that rises until it isn't worth it any more. Everything else is machinery around that one idea.
  • Five keepers a year: three regular, two rookie.
  • A keeper costs you a draft pick, not money. Keeping a player in round 4 means you don't get a 4th-round pick.
  • Nobody is kept forever. Three years and he hits a wall — unless he's your one franchise player, which buys two more and then he's gone regardless.
  • The whole design goal: reward finding someone early, punish sitting on a name.

030:08

Keepers — the engine

12 minutes · the part worth slowing down for
Land this: a keeper's price is the round you drafted him in, and it gets worse every year you hold him. Year three is always market price, which is why nobody keeps anyone forever — by then you're paying exactly what he's worth.
Year you hold himWhat he costs
Year 1the round you drafted him — or his current ADP if that's cheaper
Year 2that round minus 3 — or ADP, again whichever is cheaper
Year 3ADP. No choice. This is why year three banks you nothing
Year 4the wall — gone, unless he's your franchise player
  • You have to own the pick. If you traded away your 4th, a 4th-round keeper moves to the next-earliest round you still hold. It costs you more, not less.
  • The price follows the player, not the roster. Trade him and his price and clock go with him. Cut him and whoever claims him inherits both.
  • One franchise tag, ever, for one player: years four and five at the most expensive round you ever paid for him. Then he's gone, no exceptions.
If someone pushes back
Five keepers out of fifteen picks is way too many
Each one eats the round it costs, so five keepers means five fewer picks — and three of them get more expensive every year. In practice most people will keep two or three and let the rest go.
So I just cut a guy and re-add him to reset his price?
No. Dropping him doesn't launder anything — he carries his round and his clock to whoever claims him, including you. And you didn't need the exploit anyway: year one already gives you the cheaper of his draft round or his market price.
What stops one team hoarding all the good players?
The wall. Three years and he's off your slip regardless of how good he is. The franchise tag buys two more for exactly one player, once.
040:20

Rookies, the taxi, and the R5 price

8 minutes
Land this: two of your five slots are reserved for rookies you drafted. They cost your last picks, they have no three-year clock, and they are the best assets in the league — which is the entire reason to care about a 16-pick rookie draft.
  • Any NFL rookie you drafted counts — rookie draft or veteran draft. A class is thirty deep and the rookie draft is sixteen picks, so plenty reach the veteran board.
  • A waiver pickup does not count. He's a normal keeper on the normal clock. The cheapest permanent asset in the league shouldn't be won at auction.
  • Trading a rookie keeper kills the status — for both of you. He becomes a regular keeper at his original round with the clock back at year one.
  • Taxi: two slots, two years, rookie-draft players only. Promoting one doesn't cost him his rookie-keeper status — you never stopped holding him.
  • Drafted in the rookie draft? He has no veteran round to price against, so he keeps at R5 — flat in year one, no ADP option. That number is the most likely thing in the whole ruleset to need tuning after a season.
If someone pushes back
If veteran-draft rookies count, the rookie draft is pointless
You only get two rookie-keeper slots either way. The binding constraint is the slots, not how he got there. What a rookie pick actually buys you is first crack at the class.
No clock on rookie keepers is broken
It's two players. And you're paying your last two picks for them every single year, forever — that's a real cost once your roster is good.

The money

8 minutes
Land this: $100 to play. Then a second pot funded entirely by unspent FAAB — because there should be no free ride for quitting in November.
   
Buy-in$100pool of $800
Champion$48060%
Runner-up$20025%
Third$12015%
  • Whatever FAAB you don't spend, you owe. Grind waivers all year and you pay nothing. Check out in October sitting on $85 and you pay the most.
  • The Chase winner — best of the four teams that missed the playoffs — takes that pot, up to the third-place prize ($120).
  • Anything above that splits 60 / 20 / 10 / 10 between the champion, the runner-up, third, and the Chase winner.
The line that sells it: because third place and the Chase winner both take 10% of the overflow, once the pot clears the cap they take home exactly the same amount — at any pot size. The consolation ties a playoff finish and can never beat one.
If someone pushes back
Why was it $200 before, and why change it?
Because at $200 a bubble team in week 14 was roughly indifferent between sneaking into the bracket and missing on purpose to play for the pot — both came out near $70 of expected value. That's a tanking incentive built into the foundation. Pinned to third place it can't happen at any buy-in.
I don't want to be billed for not using waivers
Fair — but that's the point of the whole mechanism, and the ceiling is $100 which you'd rather have spent on players anyway. If the number feels heavy, the lever is the conversion rate, which is vote 05.

The votes

20 minutes · six of them · all on the front page of the app
01  Trade deadline
There isn't one. Picks and keeper rights are tradeable, so a team out of it in week 12 has real assets to sell — healthy right up until it decides a playoff seed for a team playing for nothing.
Week 10Kills late seed-buying outright, costs a lot of legitimate trading.
Week 12The usual answer. A deal still has to be made by someone who's in it.
Week 13Maximum trading, maximum fire-sale risk.
NoneDynasty convention — defensible, since the Chase and both lotteries mean nobody is truly dead in week 13.
02  Trade vetoes
The question isn't “was that fair”, it's who decides. Every league that lets the room vote finds out people veto trades that make an opponent better.
No vetoes, commissioner reverses collusion onlyBad trades stand. Two accounts run by one person don't.
League vote, 5 of 8 in 24hTraditional. Punishes creative trades more than crooked ones.
Commissioner reviews every tradePuts one person in the position of ruling on deals they're competing against.
03  Waiver tiebreaker
Two people bid the same FAAB. Sleeper gives no menu here — it breaks the tie on rolling waiver priority, seeded by draft order (whoever picks last gets the best priority), and you drop to the back of the line whenever you win a claim. Free-agent adds don't move you. So the real vote is leave it or reseed it by hand.
Leave Sleeper's rolling orderSelf-correcting, and it quietly compensates whoever draws the back of the draft — which fits a league that runs its board off a lottery anyway.
Commissioner reseeds weekly to reverse standingsHelps who needs it most. Manual work, every week, all season.
Reseed once at the midpointHalf the benefit, a fraction of the admin.
04  Does the buy-in escalate?
$100 is settled for 2026. Because the pot cap is now the third-place prize, raising the buy-in raises the consolation ceiling automatically.
Flat $100Cap stays $120. Nothing to remember.
+$10 a year2027 $110, 2029 $130. Cap rises $12 a year.
+$20 a year2027 $120, 2029 $160. Cap rises $24 a year — consolation is $192 by 2029.
An escalator is a commitment device. Easy to agree to in year one, awkward to walk back in year four when someone's circumstances have changed — say out loud now whether it can be voted down later.
05  What is a FAAB dollar worth?
Nowhere in the ruleset does this exist, and it's load-bearing. The app currently assumes $1 FAAB = $1 real — which means someone who checks out in October owes $100 on top of the buy-in, as much again as their entry.
$1 = $1Maximum bite. Pot can reach $800; the $120 cap does real work.
$1 = 25¢Softer. But that same year's pot becomes ~$52, the cap never binds, and the cap stops doing anything.
Fixed penalty insteade.g. $25 flat if you finish with more than half your budget unspent. Simplest to explain, blunt.
06  When is the FAAB bill struck?
Right now it's totted up after weeks 15–17 — so anyone sitting on $80 can dump it on garbage week-16 claims and owe nothing. That's a live loophole.
End of the regular seasonWeek 14. Closes it. One-line change in the app.
Leave it at season endAccept the dump as legal, and the pot as smaller than it looks.
070:56

What's different about year one

4 minutes
  • Nobody has keepers. Every pick tonight is live. Clocks start after this season.
  • Thirteen veteran rounds, not fourteen. Thirteen plus two rookies is fifteen players against fourteen active spots and two taxi slots — which leaves you a real promote-or-stash decision instead of a formality.
  • Both draft orders are drawn flat at random. There are no standings to weight a lottery with. From 2027 the drums are weighted and the guardrails kick in.
  • Sleeper is still set to dynasty, which is why it reads max keepers: 1. It gets switched to keeper after both drafts.

The lottery

15 minutes · do this live, do not pre-run it
Land this: the draw is reproducible. It runs off a seed the room picks, and anyone can type that seed back in afterwards and get the identical order. Nobody has to take it on trust — which is the whole reason it's built this way.
Everyone opens the app on their phone
The draw is stored server-side, not in your browser — every envelope opens on their screen at the same moment it opens on yours.
Pre-Season → Lottery → The drums
One tap from the bottom bar.
Take a seed from the room, out loud, before you type it
Somebody's jersey number, a dice roll, the last four of a phone number. Saying it first is what makes the rest verifiable.
Enter the password, enter the seed, hit Draw both orders
Both drums are drawn at once — rookie order and veteran order.
Read it out from the back
Open next reveals last pick first and works up to first choice. The room learns who's stuck at the back while the prize is still in the hat, and the last envelope is the only one anyone remembers.
Tell them the seed is on the page
Anyone can re-enter it later and reproduce the exact order. Re-drawing overwrites it, which is why the button is behind a password.
Remind everyone what the order actually is: a selection order, not a draft slot. Whoever wins first choice takes any spot on the board they want. Nobody is ever stuck picking from the 1 spot.
