The Cover Issue

Six ways to bring the look of those covers into the app, each built from this week's real numbers and the eight managers' own photos. Every cover has one job in the app, listed beside it. The wordmark is ours, set in the same spirit; none of the magazine's own marks are used.

Idea 1 · the weekly cover

Game of the Week, as the issue

Borrows
The rider who breaks the masthead (Sept 2000) and the giant thin-letter word across the middle (Sept 1992). The top band of cover lines and the barcode box with the issue date.
Where it lives
Home's Game of the Week. It replaces the current poster with a new cover every Tuesday. One manager's cutout flies through the wordmark and the other is cropped hard against the right edge. The barcode box carries the week, both records and the stakes.
What it says
Devin v Josh, both 7–5, with 36 points of playoff odds on the line. The cover lines are this week's other stories, pulled live.

Idea 2 · the recap

Buried inside: the week in stamps

Borrows
The checkerboard ground, the cut-out figure with a thick coloured keyline, and the black box cover lines stamped at angles (Oct 2003).
Where it lives
The weekly recap image on Live, the one you download for the group chat. The week's top scorer is the cut-out. Each of the five Week in Review stories becomes a stamp, and the worst news of the week sits at the bottom as "buried inside".
What it says
Week 6 as it happened: Devin's 149.9, Josh by 3.6, Clayton by 52, Brandon's lucky 125, and Thad's 31.2 on the bench.

Idea 3 · the leader

The '87 halftone

Borrows
The yellow newsprint wash, the halftone dot, the chain-link fence, and one huge red number stacked in two lines (Jan 1987).
Where it lives
The front of the top-ranked manager's card on the Managers page, and the hero of power rankings. The stacked numerals are whatever the page is about: the record here, points for, or a season ("20 / 26") on the Record book.
What it says
Clayton, 10–2, first in the league with 926 points for.

Idea 4 · the masthead

Greetings from 7½ Men

Borrows
The postcard-style wordmark with a photo inside every letter, the bare concrete set, and the handwritten marker line along the bottom (Feb 1988).
Where it lives
The masthead of the Managers page and the Record book, with each letter filled by a different manager's photo, rotating weekly. The marker line is a quote from the group chat (Beeper can supply it) or a line the commissioner types in.
What it says
Last place pleading with first place: Lucas (2–10) and Clayton (10–2), with the dates that matter this month along the top.

Idea 5 · the Chase

The Chase is real

Borrows
A gritty black-and-white photo, one hot masthead colour, and a big white script headline with small caps around it (July 2017).
Where it lives
The hero of the new Chase page, starring whichever team sits last. In weeks 14 to 17 it becomes the Chase leader, and the barcode box always carries the money and the lottery balls.
What it says
Lucas at 2–10, playing for a $120 pot, and 15 balls instead of 24 if he wins it.

Idea 6 · the Wire

Steal of the week

Borrows
The sky-blue ground, a figure mid-air, the wide "XXL" lettering, a vertical rail of cover text up the left edge, and the giant thin word through the middle (Sept 1992).
Where it lives
The top of The Wire, ahead of the contract cards. The league's best keeper contract gets the cover, and the manager who holds it flies through it. When a waiver claim takes over first place, the cover changes to it.
What it says
Kenneth Walker III keeps at R3 for Clayton against an R1 market, worth +60.

Each cover is made from things the app already has: the cutouts and photos in assets/managers, the weekly stakes, Week in Review, the Chase page and the contract board. That makes any of them a CSS-and-markup change rather than new data work. Ideas 1 and 2 are the strongest pair, because they turn the two things people open every week into something worth posting.
